   >> Short answer: Archer depth yes (later), JUP V1 orders no, and the routing idea is good but it
      becomes a much smaller job once we have Archer's book.

      JUP V1: don't show them. They are not resting liquidity anyone can trade against. A V1 order
      is a trigger: when the market reaches its price, Jupiter's keeper sells/buys it by SWAPPING
      through Jupiter (which includes Manifest). So nobody can "fill" a V1 order the way you fill
      a Manifest order. Showing them as book levels would mislead people. Also V1 is deprecated
      and its book is mostly stale orders from 2024–25 (we measured ~78 tx/hour). The one useful
      insight in your idea does hold, though: a V1 sell triggering at $121 would sell into the best
      bids at that moment, which can be YOUR Manifest bid. That's a nice-to-have "pending
      triggers" indicator, not book depth. Low priority.

      Archer: yes, showing its depth (with an "A" badge) is useful for exactly your reason: seeing
      where the real market makers sit so you can price just ahead of them on Manifest. Two
      caveats from research:
        - Archer settles trades in discrete batch auctions, not continuously, so its quotes are
          more "indicative" than Manifest's.
        - We still need a way to read its book. Earlier research found its API needs a key; the
          next step is checking whether its on-chain accounts can be decoded directly (no key).
      Your routing example (buy 3 SOL at $123: take $121 on Manifest + $122 on Archer, rest 1 SOL
      at $123 on Manifest) is a good design, and the Manifest half is already what our limit
      orders do. A limit buy at $123 takes every Manifest ask ≤ $123 at THEIR prices, then rests
      the remainder. Adding Archer means: before placing, take the Archer asks ≤ your limit via
      Jupiter's swap API restricted to Archer, then place the rest as a normal order. One wallet
      approval can cover both. I'd build it in this order: (1) read Archer's book + show it,
      (2) "take from Archer first" inside the limit-order flow. It isn't built yet: it needs the
      Archer data access sorted out first. Want me to start on (1)?
